## The problem we are working on

Speech recognition can produce a sentence that looks fluent but contains a
word the speaker did not intend. Because the result is plausible, the error may
be harder to notice than a visibly broken sentence. It can also change the
meaning.

Voiceitt does the essential first job of recognizing speech and turning it into
text. Sayloom is being developed as a companion for the next step. It receives
the text and suggests limited corrections to words that may have been
misrecognized. The person can review the suggestion, change the original text,
or keep the original wording.

Sayloom does not listen to the speech or check a suggestion against a
recording. It works only from the text it receives, the surrounding words, any
temporary context the person chooses to provide, and personal terms or known
confusions the person has explicitly approved.

Sayloom is independent and unaffiliated with Voiceitt. It is not part of the
Voiceitt company or product, and this proposed tryout is not a Voiceitt program
or an official Voiceitt integration. Sayloom is designed to complement
Voiceitt, not replace it.

## Why we are starting with a small tryout

The current work is not a broad participant study. It first develops and checks
a reusable Smart-correction “recipe.” The recipe is the common set of rules
Sayloom follows when it considers a correction. Those rules are meant to limit
unnecessary rewriting, protect the apparent meaning, and leave uncertain words
unchanged rather than guess.

The first checks use one person's private text to answer whether a fixed recipe
is safe and useful enough to take to the next stage. The recipe can be shared,
but that person's sentences, preferred terms, and known speech-recognition
confusions are not part of it.

If the recipe passes its first-user checks, that result will apply only to the
first user. It will not show that Sayloom works for other people. A later
participant tryout is how we will begin to learn whether the approach is clear,
respectful, and potentially useful to others.

## Who may be a good fit

We would like to hear from:

- people with non-standard speech who use Voiceitt to produce text;
- family members or support people, when the Voiceitt user wants them involved;
- accessibility professionals who understand communication workflows; and
- community organizations interested in helping shape a respectful future
  tryout.

The person whose words are being corrected remains the authority on what they
meant. A support person may help with access, navigation, or communication when
invited, but does not decide whether a suggestion is correct unless the
participant asks them to do so.

## What a later tryout would involve

The tryout is planned as a short, guided use of an ordinary, non-sensitive
sentence. It follows this path:

1. **Speak.** Use Voiceitt in the usual way to turn speech into text. The text
   is then placed into Sayloom. Depending on the tryout setup, this may use the
   normal Voiceitt browser workflow or a clearly labeled copy-and-paste step.
2. **Review.** Sayloom shows the text it received and one prepared version. Any
   added, removed, or replaced words are made visible. If the suggestion is
   wrong, the participant can edit the received text and ask Sayloom to try
   again. Keeping the original is always an option. Confirming that a correction
   looks right is a separate, deliberate action.
3. **Remember.** After a correction is confirmed, Sayloom may offer a personal
   preferred term or a known confusion to remember when the change looks as
   though it may recur. At this point it is only a proposal and is marked as not
   saved. The participant can decline it, edit it, or explicitly approve it.
   Nothing is learned or saved automatically.
4. **Copy.** The participant can copy the exact version they prefer. Copying
   does not mean that they agree with a correction and does not save it. Nothing
   is sent to another application automatically.

The learning loop is **Confirm → Approve → Next time**. If an approved term or
confusion is relevant to similar text later, Sayloom may use it as evidence for
a new suggestion. It is not an unconditional replacement rule. The participant
reviews the new suggestion and remains in control each time.

## The participant remains in charge

Sayloom proposes wording. It does not know for certain what someone said or
meant, and a confident-looking suggestion is not proof.

During the tryout, the participant can:

- compare the received and prepared text;
- see which words Sayloom changed;
- edit the received text and try again;
- choose the original or prepared text to copy;
- confirm or decline each correction separately from copying;
- approve, edit, or decline anything Sayloom offers to remember; and
- stop the tryout or skip a question.

Participation should not depend on agreeing with Sayloom. A wrong or confusing
suggestion is useful feedback, not a participant mistake.

## What is and is not saved

The participant tryout is for text correction, not speech or voice research.

### Sayloom does not collect

- source audio or Voiceitt recordings for this text-only correction work;
- information automatically “learned” from using, copying, or confirming text;
- personal terms or known confusions that the participant has not explicitly
  approved;
- one participant's approved information for use in another person's
  corrections; or
- a participant's sentence for later research or demonstration without
  separate, explicit consent.

### Sayloom may use during the tryout

- the text received from Voiceitt;
- the prepared text Sayloom suggests;
- optional context the participant chooses to provide for that sentence; and
- that participant's own approved terms or known confusions, if they have
  chosen to use them.

The received text, prepared text, and optional context are working information
for the correction. They are not a personal text history. The exact system used
for the tryout, any outside text-processing provider, its privacy terms, and how
working text is deleted will be disclosed before consent. If those protections
cannot be confirmed, the tryout should not proceed.

### Sayloom saves only with a clear reason

If the participant explicitly approves a personal term or known confusion,
Sayloom may save that item in a profile belonging only to that participant. The
saved item is meant to help with similar future text. It does not include source
audio and does not train Voiceitt or a shared model.

## What feedback we will ask for

After the guided sentence, we expect to ask questions such as:

- Did the suggestion match what you meant?
- Was it clear what Sayloom changed?
- Was it clear that copying, confirming, and approving a saved item were
  separate choices?
- Did you understand what Sayloom could remember and what it would not save?
- Did any step feel demanding, rushed, confusing, or inaccessible?
- Would an approved correction reduce repeated effort in your usual writing?
- What would need to change before you would feel comfortable using this again?

This feedback will help identify harmful or confusing suggestions, unclear
controls, accessibility barriers, privacy concerns, and parts of the process
that require too much effort. It will guide whether Sayloom should move forward,
be revised, or stop. Participation is not an endorsement, and positive feedback
will not be treated as proof that the product works broadly.

## What we can and cannot claim

Before a participant tryout begins, the correction recipe is expected to have
passed a private first-user review. That would support testing the next
question; it would not establish effectiveness for Voiceitt users generally.

At this stage:

- Sayloom has not been validated by prospective partner participants;
- no community organization or participant should be described as a partner,
  sponsor, or endorser without a separate agreement;
- there is no claim that Sayloom will always identify the intended word, reduce
  effort for every user, or be ready for independent everyday use;
- the participant workflow remains a later stage after recipe validation and
  may change before recruitment begins; and
- Sayloom should not be described as integrated with, approved by, or affiliated
  with Voiceitt.

The purpose of inviting participants is to gather that missing evidence, not to
present it as already established.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Sayloom replace Voiceitt?

No. Voiceitt turns speech into text. Sayloom works after that step and considers
limited corrections to the resulting text.

### Does Sayloom hear or record my voice?

No. Sayloom receives text, not source audio, for this correction work. It cannot
compare its suggestion with what was spoken.

### Who decides what I meant?

You do. Sayloom can suggest wording, but you can reject it, edit the received
text, or keep the original.

### Does using or copying a suggestion teach Sayloom automatically?

No. Copying and confirming are separate actions, and neither automatically
saves a personal rule. Sayloom saves a preferred term or known confusion only
after you explicitly approve that item.

### Will my approved information affect someone else?

No. Personal approved terms and known confusions remain isolated to the person
who approved them.

### Do I need to share a private or important sentence?

No. The guided tryout should use an ordinary, non-sensitive sentence. You can
skip a sentence or stop if something feels too personal.

### Is Sayloom already proven to work?

No. The first-user checks evaluate whether one correction recipe is ready for a
later participant tryout. They do not prove benefit for other people. The
tryout is intended to learn what works, what does not, and what must change.

### Is this an official Voiceitt study or partnership?

No. Sayloom is independent and unaffiliated with Voiceitt.

### Can I ask for access support or involve someone I trust?

Yes. Tell **[accessibility contact]** what would help. You may involve a family
member, support person, or communication partner if you choose.
